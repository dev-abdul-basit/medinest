Hook is technical, not personal. Caregiving angle appears as evidence in the middle, not the lead.

A year of building Medinest taught me 3 things about designing for medication adherence — and almost none of them were technical. Medinest is a Flutter pill reminder & medication journal I built solo, now live on Google Play in 50+ languages. The original brief was personal: [relative] is on [N] daily medications, and the off-the-shelf reminder apps were failing in three predictable ways. Here's what those three failures taught me: → Louder alarms train the user to swipe faster, not respond faster. The fix was a full-screen reminder that requires a tap to dismiss. Friction as a feature when the goal is trust. → A checklist UI feels like a chore. People don't want to "tick boxes" — they want to not have to. The fix was a single "Taken" button on home, no list to navigate. → The doctor visit always ends with "and have you been taking your medicine?" — and nobody has a clean answer. The fix isn't a tracker, it's a journal. A note like "switched to evening dose, less nausea" attached to that medicine, visible six months later. That's the feature Medinest gets the most quiet thank-yous for, and the one that's hardest to advertise. Lessons that compounded across all three: — Designing for the caregiver matters more than designing for the user. The person who installs the app is rarely the person taking the medicine. — Boring features (notes, profile-switching, sound choice) drive far more retention than the cool features (graphs, AI, social). — The hardest week was the week I stopped adding features and started removing them. Currently __k+ users, free, Android-first, premium at 5 SAR/mo for the MENA market. Week 2 — Tuesday — Pricing post (~1,300 chars)

I priced Medinest's premium plan at 5 SAR/month. That's about $1.30. Let me explain why. Saudi Arabia is Medinest's primary market. Setting prices in SAR — and not auto-converting from USD — has lifted MENA conversion 3× compared to a USD-anchored price. The trade-off: when 5 SAR auto-converts to USD for a US user, Medinest looks suspiciously cheap next to Medisafe at $4.99/mo. The fix is Play Console regional pricing — a price tier per region, not a single global one. Most indie devs skip this step because Play Console buries it. It's one of the highest-leverage 30-min jobs in mobile pricing. A pill reminder for $1.30/mo in Riyadh and $4.99/mo in Chicago isn't pricing chaos. It's purchasing-power parity, which is what every category leader does and most solo devs forget. If you're an indie dev shipping a freemium app and pricing in one currency for the world, you're leaving 30–60% of revenue on the floor. #Flutter #ProductPricing #IndieDev #KSA #BuildInPublic

Week 3 — Tuesday — AI restraint post (~1,800 chars)

Why I almost added AI to Medinest, and why I pulled back. Six months ago "AI medication assistant" felt inevitable. Every healthtech investor I bumped into asked when Medinest would have it. Two issues stopped me: First — regulatory. Even a soft "AI suggests when to take your dose" feature crosses a line that triggers Play Store medical-device policy review. Most apps get through eventually. Some don't. The cost of getting it wrong, for a one-person operation, is the company. Second — actual user need. I shadowed [N] caregivers in Riyadh for a few hours each. Not one said "I wish my reminder app understood me better." All of them said variants of "I wish my reminder app would just not fail." That's a notification reliability problem, not an AI problem. What I built instead: → Full-screen reminders that require a tap to dismiss → Per-medicine custom sounds (11 of them — sound choice is more product than I thought) → Family-profile reminders that route to the right person → Journal notes pinned to specific medicines, visible at the next doctor visit The "AI feature" I might ship eventually: an opt-in summary of medication notes for the next doctor visit. Local processing only. No diagnosis, no advice. That's a writing-aid feature, not an AI agent. If you're building healthtech and an investor asks where your AI story is — be ready with an answer that protects your audience, not your fundraising. #Flutter #Healthcare #AIEthics #ProductDecisions
