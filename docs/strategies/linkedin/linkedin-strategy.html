LinkedIn Strategy — MediNest

LinkedIn audience is not the user. The user is on X / Reddit / Play search. LinkedIn audience is decision-makers, healthtech operators, B2B partners, and journalists who write app roundups. Treat it as PR + partnership channel, not direct user acquisition.

If you measure LinkedIn by installs, it will look weak. The right measures are: press mentions, partnership inbounds, hires (if you ever hire), and the time it cuts off your due-diligence cycle when someone is evaluating you.

Two accounts, two purposes

You will operate both:

  1. Your personal profile — where the actual content goes. LinkedIn distribution favors people, not pages, by 5–20×.
  2. MediNest company page — exists for credibility (logo, link in posts, employees can claim it later). Posts there get almost no organic reach. Use sparingly.

Default: post from personal, tag the company page, repost to the company page after 24 hours.

Your personal profile setup

Headline (≤220 chars, indexed in LinkedIn search)

Building MediNest — pill reminder & medication tracker for caregivers. Solo developer · Flutter · 50+ languages · 10k+ users

Replace user count with what's true.

About section (story arc, not feature list)

3 paragraphs:

  1. Why you built it (one specific story — you, a parent, a patient you know).
  2. What it is, what it isn't (avoids medical-claims trap, signals seriousness).
  3. The current state — version, languages, install band, premium price. Numbers signal confidence.

End with: "DMs open. If you work in caregiver tech, polypharmacy, or healthtech distribution, I'd love to compare notes."

What to post — content pillars

Pillar A — Founder's POV on healthtech (the strongest pillar on LinkedIn)

LinkedIn rewards "I have a non-obvious view because I shipped something." Examples:

Most pill-reminder apps optimize for the user. I've started optimizing for the caregiver — the person setting up the app for someone else. The flows are completely different. Two examples…

We talk a lot about "AI in healthcare" on this platform. I built a Health & Fitness app and intentionally did not add AI. Here's why my decision was not technological — it was regulatory and ethical.

Pillar B — Build journey, business view

On X this is "I shipped this feature." On LinkedIn it's "I shipped this feature and here's what I learned about pricing / market / users." Always include a number.

Pillar C — Caregiver advocacy

Adjacent posts that don't sell the app at all but signal that you understand the audience. Highest reach but lowest direct conversion. Worth doing for trust.

Pillar D — Reply-style commentary on big healthtech posts

Find recent posts from Olivier Verbeke (caregiver tech), Drew Schiller, Bertalan Mesko, Natasha Bhuyan, large healthtech VCs. Don't reply with "great post" — reply with one sharp counter-example or extension. This grows your in-network reach faster than any post.

Anti-pillars

  • Hustle culture content (LinkedIn punishes it less than X but our audience punishes it harder)
  • Generic AI takes
  • "Just shipped! 🎉" — dress these up with at least one number and one lesson
  • Anything without a clear single takeaway

What you do NOT do on LinkedIn

  • Buy "LinkedIn growth" services. They get accounts restricted.
  • Run automated DM tools (Dux-Soup, Phantombuster). Same.
  • Post the same content as X verbatim. LinkedIn audience reads more text and expects more substance.
  • Use LinkedIn polls except when you genuinely want the answer. They get reach but cheapen the profile.

DM outreach — slow and sharp

These targets, prioritized:

  1. Healthtech newsletter writers who do app roundups (e.g., HISTalk, MM&M's writers, Andre Blackman, Gil Bashe contacts). Pitch: a 3-line email-style DM. Goal: get on a watchlist.
  2. Caregiver-org community managers at AARP, AgingLife Care Association, country-specific equivalents. Pitch: free Premium for the community + content collaboration.
  3. Healthtech VCs — DO NOT pitch for funding (pill-reminder is not VC-fundable). DO ping for introductions to portfolio companies that might cross-promote.
  4. Pharma DTC marketers — risky territory but real opportunity. Pharma can't easily distribute their own reminder app due to compliance. We can. Some will license / co-brand.
  5. Other indie developers in adjacent spaces — sleep, water, period tracking — for cross-promotion.

DM template (adapt always)

Hi {Name},

Read your piece on {specific thing they wrote about}. The bit on {specific point} matched something I'm seeing with MediNest — a pill reminder app I'm building. {One concrete observation, 1 sentence.}

Not pitching, just curious if you'd be open to a 15-min compare-notes call. Happy to do async if that's easier.

— Abdul

The first DM is never asking for anything. Ever.

Press / app-roundup angle

Pitch one journalist per week starting Week 4. Categories that publish app roundups:

  • Mainstream tech: TechCrunch, The Verge, Engadget — low hit rate, not worth time unless we have a hook.
  • Health-adjacent tech: Wareable, HealthITNews, MobiHealthNews — much better hit rate.
  • Lifestyle pubs: Real Simple, Family Circle, AARP, country-specific senior magazines — best hit rate, especially around "best apps for caregivers" listicles.
  • Country-specific: Saudi Tech Hub equivalents, Indian tech press, Indonesian tech press — much higher response rate, especially after we have localized listings.

Press pitch one-liner (use as DM subject)

Solo developer ships pill reminder app in 50+ languages — angle on accessibility / caregiving for [outlet's specific audience]

Why LinkedIn is worth less time than X for this app

Be honest about prioritization. LinkedIn is the 30 % channel, not the 50 % channel. Time budget:

  • X: 5–7 hours/week
  • Reddit: 3–4 hours/week
  • LinkedIn: 2–3 hours/week
  • Everything else: 1 hour/week

If LinkedIn ever becomes a primary install channel for a pill-reminder app, something has gone weird with the LinkedIn algorithm and we should re-evaluate.
